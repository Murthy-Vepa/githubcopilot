The Week 1 decisions

Decision 01

Approve the SDD operating model

Agree that delivery begins with intent and a problem statement, not a code request. The minimum path is:

  1. Intent and problem statement
  2. Requirements and acceptance criteria
  3. Specification and constraints
  4. Implementation plan and task breakdown
  5. Implementation, tests, review, and evidence

For each stage, name the responsible role, the reviewer, the required artifact, and the point at which work may proceed.

Decision 02

Make accountability explicit

Copilot and agents may help explore, draft, transform, and check work. They do not approve requirements, architecture, security decisions, test adequacy, acceptance, or release.

  • A product or business owner confirms the outcome and priority.
  • A delivery or technical owner confirms feasibility and design.
  • Reviewers validate security, privacy, accessibility, testing, and operational impact.
  • The release owner accepts the final evidence and known limitations.

When SDD is required

Required

Use the complete SDD path for work that changes business behavior or carries meaningful risk.

  • New features, services, or user journeys
  • Material changes to existing behavior or integrations
  • Customer-facing, regulated, security, privacy, or compliance work
  • Changes involving sensitive data, authorization, or production operations
  • Cross-team work with significant dependencies or architectural impact

Scaled SDD

Use the same intent, requirements, review, and evidence principles with fewer artifacts when the change is small and low risk.

  • Small enhancements with clear acceptance criteria
  • Routine maintenance with limited behavior change
  • Internal tooling changes with an identified owner and test evidence

The team records which artifacts were combined and why.

Exception candidates

An exception is a controlled decision, not an absence of process.

  • Emergency production remediation
  • Minor typo, documentation, or formatting changes
  • Routine configuration or dependency updates with established checks
  • Time-critical operational actions where delay creates greater risk

Post-incident documentation and retrospective traceability may still be required.

Exception record

Record before bypassing the standard

Use an exception record in the issue, change ticket, or approved repository location. Keep it short enough to complete during delivery.

  • Work item and requesting team
  • SDD stage or artifact being skipped or combined
  • Reason and urgency
  • Risk introduced and compensating checks
  • Named approver and accountable owner
  • Expiry date, follow-up action, or retrospective review

Approval rule

Exceptions must be visible, justified, owned, and time-bound. An emergency exception may be approved after the change, but the approver and follow-up deadline must still be recorded.

Escalate when:

the change affects sensitive data, security controls, customer commitments, regulatory obligations, or a shared production dependency.

Traceability and quality gates

Connect every decision

Each artifact should link to the item before it and the evidence after it:

REQ-###->SPEC-###->PLAN-###->TASK-###->PR / TEST

Use the team's approved issue, pull request, document, or folder convention. The identifier matters less than making the relationship easy to follow.

Pause at the gates

  • Intent gate: the problem and outcome are understood.
  • Requirements gate: acceptance criteria are clear and testable.
  • Specification gate: design, risks, and constraints are reviewed.
  • Plan gate: tasks, dependencies, and test approach are credible.
  • Acceptance gate: implementation, tests, review, and limitations are evidenced.

Definition of Ready and Definition of Done

DoR / Before work starts

A team may start when

  • The user, problem, and intended outcome are clear.
  • Scope, exclusions, stakeholders, assumptions, and dependencies are recorded.
  • Requirements have testable acceptance criteria.
  • Security, privacy, accessibility, data, and operational concerns have been considered.
  • The work has an owner, priority, and appropriate SDD path.
  • Required approvals or an explicit approved exception are present.
DoD / Before acceptance

A team may call it done when

  • Implementation satisfies the approved requirements and specification.
  • Automated or manual tests provide evidence for the acceptance criteria.
  • Code review and required security, privacy, accessibility, and quality checks are complete.
  • Traceability links connect requirements to tasks, changes, tests, and evidence.
  • Documentation, monitoring, rollout, and rollback needs are addressed.
  • Known limitations, exceptions, and follow-up work are recorded and owned.

Hands-on exercise

Turn a vague request into a responsible starting point

Take a real or representative request such as “improve the claims search experience.” Record the user, outcome, business constraints, non-functional concerns, data considerations, dependencies, and what would make the result acceptable. Do not ask Copilot to write code yet. The goal is to establish enough context for a useful specification.

Completion checklist

  • Problem and outcome are stated in plain language.
  • Stakeholders and assumptions are named.
  • Security, privacy, accessibility, and operational concerns are listed.
  • Team agrees what evidence will be reviewed.
Facilitator emphasis

Week 1 is successful when teams understand that Copilot is part of an engineering system, not a substitute for problem definition, design judgment, testing, or accountability. Keep the examples concrete and let teams surface exceptions early.

Required deliverables and readiness gate

Submit by Friday

  • Team charter with owner, participants, support channel, and selected story.
  • Approved SDD workflow with required-use and exception rules.
  • Published requirements, specification, plan, task, exception, and evidence templates.
  • Traceability convention and DoR / DoD checklists.
  • One-page problem statement, draft requirements, and initial specification outline.
  • Completed access and repository readiness checklist.
  • Open questions or exception requests for the enablement team.

Ready for Week 2 when

  • All participants can access the required tools, repositories, and support channels.
  • The selected story is low-risk and small enough for guided practice.
  • The team can explain the SDD stages, review points, and human accountability.
  • The team knows when to use full SDD, scaled SDD, or an exception.
  • The team can show how a requirement will connect to implementation and evidence.
  • No unresolved security or data-handling blocker remains.
