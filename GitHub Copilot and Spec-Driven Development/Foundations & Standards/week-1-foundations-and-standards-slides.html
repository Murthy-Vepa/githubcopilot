GitHub Copilot / SDD01 / 15
Week 1 / Setting the foundation

From AI enthusiasm to an accountable way of working.

Establish the intent, standards, and shared language that make GitHub Copilot adoption useful, responsible, and repeatable.

AI adoption + Spec-Driven Development
01 / Adoption intent

Adoption is a change in the system, not a new shortcut.

Faster learning

Use Copilot to explore, explain, draft, transform, and test while keeping engineering judgment in the loop.

Better delivery

Start with a clear outcome and constraints so generated work is connected to a real problem.

Stronger trust

Make review, evidence, ownership, and known limitations visible before work reaches customers.

Intent: increase capability without lowering the quality bar.
02 / Mindset shift

Move from “generate code” to “shape and verify outcomes.”

Old instinct

  • Begin with a code request.
  • Trust a plausible answer too quickly.
  • Discover requirements during implementation.
  • Treat review as a final inspection.

Operating expectation

  • Begin with intent and a problem statement.
  • Ask for evidence, alternatives, and limitations.
  • Make requirements and constraints explicit.
  • Review decisions at the point they matter.
Human accountability remains constant: Copilot can assist with work; accountable people approve it.

04 / Intent to evidence

Every stage answers a different question.

Intent->Requirements->Specification->Plan->Tasks->Implementation->Tests + review->Evidence

Intent makes the why visible

State the user, problem, desired outcome, scope, stakeholders, constraints, assumptions, and risks before proposing a solution.

Evidence makes the result trustworthy

Connect implementation to requirements through tests, review links, checks, known limitations, and acceptance ownership.

05 / Required-use standard

Use the complete SDD path when change or risk is meaningful.

Full SDD is required for

  • New features, services, or user journeys.
  • Material behavior or integration changes.
  • Customer-facing, regulated, security, privacy, or compliance work.
  • Sensitive data, authorization, or production operations.
  • Cross-team or architecturally significant work.

The decision rule

When uncertainty, impact, or reversibility is low, use the full path. The owner records the decision and the evidence expected at each gate.

Default:When in doubt, use SDD and ask the accountable reviewer to scale it deliberately.
06 / Proportionate control

Scale the paperwork, never the accountability.

Scaled SDD

For small, low-risk work, combine artifacts while retaining intent, acceptance, review, ownership, and evidence.

Exception candidates

Emergency remediation, minor documentation changes, routine updates with established checks, or time-critical operations.

Still required

Record what was skipped or combined, why, the risk, compensating checks, approver, owner, and follow-up date.

An exception is a controlled decision, not an absence of process.
07 / Exception record

Make the deviation visible, justified, and time-bound.

Minimum record

  • Work item and requesting team.
  • Stage or artifact skipped or combined.
  • Reason and urgency.
  • Risk introduced and compensating checks.
  • Named approver and accountable owner.
  • Expiry date, follow-up action, or retrospective review.

Escalate when the change affects

  • Sensitive data or security controls.
  • Customer commitments or regulatory obligations.
  • A shared production dependency.
Emergency rule:Approval may follow the change, but the approver and follow-up deadline must still be recorded.
08 / Standards approval

Approve the templates before asking teams to use them.

Problem statement: outcome before solution Requirements: what must be true Specification: behavior and boundaries Plan: safe implementation approach Tasks: executable, linked work Evidence: tests, review, and limitations
Approval signal:Business, technical, security, and delivery owners know what each artifact is for, what “good” looks like, and who can approve it.
09 / Follow the decision trail

Traceability turns a collection of files into a defensible delivery story.

REQ-###->SPEC-###->PLAN-###->TASK-###->PR / TEST

Every link should answer

Which requirement does this change satisfy? Which design decision shaped it? Which task delivered it? Which test or review proves it?

Use local conventions

The identifier format matters less than making relationships easy to follow in the approved issue, repository, pull request, or document system.

10 / Pause points

Move forward when the next decision is ready to be made.

1

Intent: problem, outcome, and stakeholders are understood.

2

Requirements: acceptance criteria are clear and testable.

3

Specification: design, risks, and constraints are reviewed.

4

Plan: tasks, dependencies, and test approach are credible.

5

Acceptance: implementation, tests, review, and limitations are evidenced.

11 / DoR

Ready means the team can begin without inventing the problem.

Before work starts

  • User, problem, and intended outcome are clear.
  • Scope, exclusions, stakeholders, assumptions, and dependencies are recorded.
  • Requirements have testable acceptance criteria.
  • Security, privacy, accessibility, data, and operational concerns are considered.
  • Owner, priority, and SDD path are explicit.
  • Approvals or an approved exception are present.

DoR is a quality decision

It protects delivery time by making ambiguity visible before implementation begins. A task can be urgent without being ready; urgency should trigger an explicit exception, not hidden assumptions.

12 / DoD

Done means the result is accepted, evidenced, and owned.

Before acceptance

  • Implementation satisfies the approved requirements and specification.
  • Tests provide evidence for acceptance criteria.
  • Code review and required security, privacy, accessibility, and quality checks are complete.
  • Traceability links connect requirements to changes, tests, and evidence.
  • Documentation, monitoring, rollout, and rollback needs are addressed.

Close the loop

  • Known limitations are recorded and owned.
  • Exceptions have a follow-up date or closure decision.
  • The acceptance or release owner confirms the evidence is sufficient.
Copilot does not declare done.People review the evidence and accept the remaining risk.
13 / Commit to the standard

Very soon, each team should have a usable starting point.

Approved workflow

Required-use rules, scaled SDD guidance, exception record, quality gates, and named owners.

Approved templates

Problem statement, requirements, specification, plan, tasks, evidence, traceability, DoR, and DoD.

First candidate story

Low-risk problem statement, draft requirements, initial specification outline, and access readiness.

14 / Close

Use AI to extend judgment, not to replace it.

The standard is simple to state and serious to practice: define the outcome, make the decisions visible, review the work, and leave evidence that another person can trust.
